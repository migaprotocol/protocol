## Mission & Vision

### Mission Statement

To build decentralized financial and governance infrastructure that enables the global Persian community to coordinate capital, govern collectively, and fund humanitarian initiatives with full transparency and privacy protection.

### Vision Statement

A world where stateless nations and diaspora communities have access to the same tools of collective organization as nation-states—transparent treasuries, democratic governance, and sustainable funding mechanisms—without requiring permission from any central authority.

### Core Values

| Value | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Transparency** | Every transaction visible, every decision auditable |
| **Privacy** | Contributor identities protected, especially in high-threat environments |
| **Decentralization** | No single point of failure or control |
| **Sustainability** | Self-funding mechanisms that don't depend on continuous fundraising |
| **Inclusivity** | Accessible to Persians everywhere, regardless of location or wealth |

## Problem Analysis

### The Persian Diaspora Challenge

**Demographics:**
- 88 million Iranians (population of Iran)
- 8+ million Persian diaspora globally
- Major concentrations: USA (1.5M), Canada (400K), Germany (300K), UK (250K), UAE (400K), Australia (100K)

**Economic Profile:**
- Iranian-Americans: Median household income $87,000 (vs. $65,000 US average)
- 57% hold bachelor's degrees or higher
- Disproportionate representation in medicine, engineering, entrepreneurship
- Estimated diaspora wealth: $300B+

**Current Pain Points:**

| Problem | Impact | Current Solutions | Limitations |
|---------|--------|-------------------|-------------|
| Fragmented giving | Inefficient capital allocation | Individual charities | No coordination, high overhead |
| Remittance friction | 10-15% lost to fees/FX | Hawala, informal channels | Legal risk, no transparency |
| No collective voice | Political marginalization | Diaspora organizations | Centralized, often politicized |
| Sanctions barriers | Exclusion from financial system | Workarounds | Compliance risk |
| Safety concerns | Risk to family in Iran | Anonymity | Limits participation |

### The Humanitarian Gap

Annual humanitarian needs in/around Iran:
- Medical supplies and equipment
- Educational resources
- Disaster relief
- Civil society support
- Independent media funding

**Current annual flows:** $500M+ through fragmented NGOs
**Estimated unmet need:** $2B+
**Key barrier:** Lack of trusted, transparent, efficient infrastructure

## Business Model

### Revenue Model

MIGA operates as a non-profit protocol. All revenues accrue to the DAO treasury for community-governed allocation.

**Revenue Streams:**

| Stream | Mechanism | Annual Estimate |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| Pre-Sale | One-time contributions | $1-5M |
| Trading Fees | 0.3% on Meteora pools (10% to DAO) | $100K-500K |
| Bridge Fees | 0.1% on cross-chain transfers | $50K-200K |
| Protocol Services | Premium features, API access | $50K-100K |
| Treasury Yield | DeFi strategies on idle funds | $200K-1M |

**Cost Structure:**

| Category | Annual Estimate | Notes |
|----------|-----------------|-------|
| Infrastructure | $50K-100K | Servers, RPCs, services |
| Security | $100K-200K | Audits, bug bounties |
| Development | $0 (grants) | Funded through DAO grants |
| Marketing | $50K-100K | Community-driven |
| Legal | $50K-100K | Compliance, structure |
| **Total** | **$250K-500K** | |

**Sustainability:**

At steady state, protocol fees + treasury yield should exceed operating costs, creating perpetual sustainability without requiring additional fundraising.

## Competitive Analysis

### SWOT Analysis

**Strengths:**
- First mover in Persian diaspora crypto
- Zero insider allocation builds trust
- Multi-chain from genesis
- Strong technical architecture
- Quantum-safe privacy

**Weaknesses:**
- No established track record
- Regulatory uncertainty
- Dependence on crypto market conditions
- Complex product requiring education

**Opportunities:**
- Untapped $300B+ diaspora market
- Growing crypto adoption globally
- Increasing interest in network states
- Mainstream media attention on Iran

**Threats:**
- Regulatory crackdown on crypto
- Sanctions complications
- Competition from well-funded entrants
- Crypto market downturn

| Moat | Description | Defensibility |
